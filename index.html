✦ The three parts

Roughly forty minutes each

Split where the subject splits: what a token is, how it is protected, and how the protection is built.

1
Chapters 1 – 3 JWT, Piece by Piece What a token is, using a concert wristband. Why they exist, using a coat check. The three pieces, the seven claims that read like a railway ticket, and the three ways people break the whole thing. You will decode a token by clicking it, run a session simulator, play attacker in three labs, and build a token from JSON. Read part one →
2
Chapters 4 – 5 The Seal and the Envelope A rubber stamp everyone can copy versus a signature only you can write. Then the reversal that catches everybody: encryption runs the opposite way round, and does not replace signing. You will generate real key pairs, fail to forge with a public key, and really encrypt something, then break the seal and watch it refuse to open. Read part two →
3
Chapters 6 – 8 The Locksmith's Manual Everything the first two parts labelled "the maths" and skipped. Base64 to the bit, hashing, HMAC, RSA and elliptic curves, then six real attacks and the eight rules that stop them. You will watch one letter move half a hash, build HMAC by hand, break an RSA key, and draw on an elliptic curve. Read part three →

Recreated by Swaraj Bangar❤️

Based on The JWT Handbook, version 0.14.2, by Sebastián E. Peyrott, published by Auth0, the source of the structure, the specifications and the examples throughout. The best-practice chapter draws on the IETF OAuth Working Group's JWT Best Current Practices draft. The wristbands, coat checks, railway tickets, rubber stamps, suggestion boxes, locked trunks and signature books are these pages' own, added so the ideas land without a background in cryptography.

Every cryptographic operation across the three parts runs in your browser through Web Crypto, real HMAC, real RSA and ECDSA key pairs, real AES-GCM encryption. Nothing you type leaves the page. None of it is production code: real systems need battle-tested libraries, careful key handling and proper testing.
